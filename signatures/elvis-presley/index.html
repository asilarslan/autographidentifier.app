Elvis Presley autograph: how to tell real from fake

Secretarial signatures and printed photos account for many "signed" Elvis items.

Elvis Presley signed for fans throughout his career, on photos, album covers, scraps of paper and contracts. He died in 1977, and demand has outstripped the supply of genuine signatures ever since.

Many Elvis items sold as signed were never touched by him: they carry secretarial signatures from his office, or signatures printed on promotional photos.

What a genuine Elvis Presley signature looks like

  • Usually signed in full as Elvis Presley, with a large looping capital E and a tall P.
  • In-person signatures were often written quickly, standing up, in whatever pen a fan had. Size and neatness vary a lot from one example to the next.
  • 1950s signatures tend to be more careful and legible than hurried examples from the 1970s.

Common fakes and red flags

  • Secretarial signatures on fan mail photos, signed by staff on his behalf.
  • Printed or stamped facsimile signatures on promotional photos and souvenir items.
  • Felt-tip signatures on items claimed to be from the 1950s, before such pens were available.
  • Photos "signed" on a print that was produced after 1977.

How to verify a Elvis Presley autograph

  • Check that the item, the print and the pen all fit the claimed date.
  • Look at the signature under magnification. A printed signature is flat and made of dots; real ink has edges, overlaps and varying density.
  • For a purchase of any size, look for a letter of authenticity from an established autograph authenticator.

Elvis Presley autograph FAQ

How can I tell if my Elvis autograph is real?

Rule out a printed signature first, then check that the item is from his lifetime, then compare the handwriting with certified examples of the same period. Because secretarial signatures are common, professional authentication is recommended.

What is a secretarial Elvis signature?

A signature written by a member of staff on his behalf, typically on photos sent out in reply to fan mail. They are period items but not his autograph.

Did Elvis sign with a Sharpie?

Felt-tip markers only became common in the mid-1960s, so they are possible on later items but not on anything signed in the 1950s.

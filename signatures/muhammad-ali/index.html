Muhammad Ali autograph: how to tell real from fake

From "Cassius Clay" to the later, smaller signature, and how the eras differ.

Muhammad Ali was a famously willing signer, and he signed for fans for more than fifty years. That long span is what makes his autograph tricky: the signature changed name, size and steadiness over time.

The first question with any Ali autograph is when it is supposed to have been signed.

What a genuine Muhammad Ali signature looks like

  • Before 1964 he signed as Cassius Clay. Genuine Clay signatures are much scarcer than Ali ones and are valued accordingly.
  • Signatures from the 1960s and 1970s are large, flowing and confident, with a bold capital M and A.
  • After his Parkinson's diagnosis in 1984 the signature gradually became smaller, slower and shakier, and in later years often simplified.
  • He frequently added inscriptions, dates or small drawings for fans, particularly in earlier decades.

Common fakes and red flags

  • A bold, fluid 1970s-style signature on an item that was produced in the 1990s or 2000s.
  • "Cassius Clay" signatures on items dated after he changed his name.
  • Imitations of the later shaky signature. Genuine tremor is irregular; a drawn imitation tends to wobble evenly.

How to verify a Muhammad Ali autograph

  • Date the item first, then compare the signature with certified examples from that same period.
  • Late-career signings were often done with authentication companies present, so paperwork should exist for those items.
  • Use PSA/DNA, JSA or Beckett for a formal opinion, and check the certificate number online.

Muhammad Ali autograph FAQ

Is a Cassius Clay autograph worth more than a Muhammad Ali autograph?

Generally yes, because he used that name for a relatively short part of his career and far fewer examples survive.

Why is my Muhammad Ali autograph so shaky?

Later signatures were affected by Parkinson's disease and are noticeably smaller and less steady. That is expected for items signed from the late 1980s onward.

How do I check an Ali autograph quickly?

Photograph it clearly and compare it with authenticated examples from the same era. An AI scan can give a fast first impression and list red flags, but it does not replace a certificate.

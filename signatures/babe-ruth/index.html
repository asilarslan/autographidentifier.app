Babe Ruth autograph: how to tell real from fake

The classic vintage autograph, and a favourite target of forgers for decades.

Babe Ruth signed generously throughout his life, so genuine examples do exist on baseballs, photos, album pages, cheques and letters. He is also among the most forged names in the hobby, and a Ruth autograph should never be bought on appearance alone.

Ruth died in 1948. That date is the single most useful fact when checking an item: the pen, the ball and the paper all have to be possible for the period.

What a genuine Babe Ruth signature looks like

  • Neat, legible, evenly slanted handwriting. Ruth wrote a tidy, school-taught script and every letter of "Babe Ruth" can normally be read.
  • A distinctive capital B and R, with the two words similar in height and evenly spaced.
  • Earlier signatures often have quotation marks around "Babe". Legal documents and cheques are typically signed G. H. Ruth or George H. Ruth.
  • On single-signed baseballs the signature usually sits on the sweet spot, the narrow panel between the seams.

Common fakes and red flags

  • The wrong pen. Felt-tip markers did not exist in Ruth's lifetime, and ballpoint pens only reached American shops in late 1945, so ballpoint is plausible only on items from his final years.
  • Clubhouse signatures on team-signed balls, where an attendant signed on behalf of players.
  • Stamped or printed facsimile signatures on souvenir balls and photos.
  • Artificially aged baseballs: uniform brown toning, or ink that sits on top of the staining instead of under it.

How to verify a Babe Ruth autograph

  • Check that the ball itself is period-correct. The league president's stamp on an official ball can be dated.
  • Look at the ink under magnification. Fountain pen ink feathers slightly into old leather and paper and fades unevenly over decades.
  • Vintage signatures at this price level should carry a full letter of authenticity from PSA/DNA, JSA or Beckett, not just a sticker or a dealer certificate.

Babe Ruth autograph FAQ

How can I tell if a Babe Ruth signed baseball is real?

Start with what can be dated: the ball, the stamping and the type of pen. Then compare the handwriting with certified examples from the same years. Because Ruth is so heavily forged, a full letter from a major authenticator is expected for any sale.

Did Babe Ruth sign with quotation marks?

Often, yes. Many earlier examples are signed "Babe" Ruth with quotation marks around the nickname, while later ones usually drop them.

Are stamped Babe Ruth signatures worth anything?

Stamped and printed facsimile signatures were produced on souvenir items. They can have modest value as memorabilia, but they are not autographs and should not be priced as such.

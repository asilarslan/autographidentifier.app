Walt Disney autograph: how to tell real from fake

The famous logo is not his handwriting, and studio artists often signed for him.

Most people picture the Walt Disney company logo when they think of his signature. That logo is a stylised design. Disney's own handwriting looks different, which is the first surprise for many owners.

The second is that several studio employees were authorised to sign his name, so a period "Walt Disney" signature is quite often by someone else.

What a genuine Walt Disney signature looks like

  • His genuine signature varied over the decades, but typically shows a large looping W and a distinctive D, written quickly and with confidence.
  • It is less uniform and less decorative than the corporate logo.
  • Genuine examples appear on letters, contracts, cheques, books and inscribed photos, often with a personal inscription.

Common fakes and red flags

  • Signatures that match the company logo closely. That is a sign of a studio artist or a forger, not of Disney himself.
  • Studio-signed items. Artists including Hank Porter and Bob Moore signed Disney's name on fan cards, photos and artwork.
  • Printed signatures on fan cards and publicity items.
  • Anything dated after his death in December 1966.

How to verify a Walt Disney autograph

  • Compare with authenticated examples from the same decade, not with the logo.
  • Disney is a specialist area with dedicated experts; their opinion, or certification from PSA/DNA, JSA or Beckett, is expected for a sale.
  • Signed documents such as cheques and contracts are generally the safest category.

Walt Disney autograph FAQ

Is the Disney logo Walt Disney's real signature?

No. The logo is a stylised version created for the company. His actual signature differed from it and changed over the years.

Who signed for Walt Disney?

Studio artists were authorised to sign his name on items sent to fans. Hank Porter and Bob Moore are the best documented.

Is a studio-signed Disney item valuable?

It can have some value as vintage Disney memorabilia, but far less than an item signed by Walt Disney himself, and it should be described accurately.
